Read it this way: per rupee of user revenue, IPO investors are paying about what Meta and Google paid in 2020, or slightly less, depending on basis. Rich against global telcos, cheaper than Airtel on P/E and market cap, and below platform multiples (it reaches only their low edge under the spectrum sensitivity). With 95% of EBITDA still from the telecom subsidiary, the valuation is consistent with investors pricing Jio mainly on its telecom economics. Whether the newer businesses ever earn a platform premium is an open question.

As of 23 Oct 2026. Jio value: ₹1,119 × ~920.9 cr post-issue shares ≈ ₹10.3L cr. Multiples: ÷ Q1 FY27 EBITDA × 4 (₹83,460 cr) = 12.3×; ÷ FY26 EBITDA (₹76,255 cr) = 13.5×. Market cap ≈ EV here: RHP net debt at 30 Jun 2026 was ₹9,539 cr (₹61,372 cr borrowings less ₹51,833 cr cash), smaller than the ₹30,213 cr fresh issue, so pro-forma net financial debt is roughly nil or slightly net cash (excluding leases and spectrum dues). But treating ~₹1.05L cr of deferred spectrum dues as debt (a sensitivity, not a definitive multiple) would lift EV/EBITDA to ~14–15×. P/E is less comparable across telcos because spectrum amortisation and interest vary widely, so EBITDA is used as the main lens.
