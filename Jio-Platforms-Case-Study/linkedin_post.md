If you were in India in late 2016, you probably remember the queues.

Outside Reliance Digital stores and half the mobile shops in every colony, people stood clutching their Aadhaar cards, waiting for a free Jio SIM. Very few of them, I suspect, thought they were "switching" from Airtel or Vodafone. They weren't leaving anybody. They simply had an empty second slot in their dual-SIM phone, and Jio asked, rather politely, to fill it.

That polite little ask took Jio to roughly 100 million users in about 170 days. (Free data didn't hurt either, of course.)

I've been chewing on that moment ever since Jio Platforms opened its IPO, because I think it explains the company better than any prospectus does.

So I did something slightly obsessive. I went back through a decade of Jio's bets beyond the SIM (JioChat, JioMeet, JioPages, JioSaavn, JioPhone, JioFiber, JioHotstar and a few more) and scored every one of them.

The bets that ride on top of the connection, like phones, broadband and TV bundled into your plan, mostly worked: 4 won, 1 mixed, 1 flopped.

The bets that had to win your attention on their own? 1 won, 2 mixed, 3 flopped.

JioMeet is my favourite example. It launched in July 2020, at the peak of lockdown, when all of us were practically living on Zoom. It looked like Zoom, it worked like Zoom, and it had all of Jio's distribution behind it. What it never gave anyone was a reason to leave Zoom. Distribution can get an app installed. It can't make you open it again tomorrow.

The irony? Three months before JioMeet, Jio had already sold about 10% of itself to Meta, and JioMart soon turned up inside WhatsApp, the very messenger JioChat was built to beat. Instead of building the habit, Jio rented it.

And the one clear win outside connectivity, JioHotstar, went from about 50 million subscribers at launch to 300 million in roughly four months, most of that jump arriving during the IPL. Jio didn't build the reason to subscribe. It bought it.

Which leaves me with a one-line read of the whole strategy: Jio sells distribution and buys preference.

Jio lists on Wednesday, and most of the chatter will be about the listing pop. I'm more curious about the next decade. When AI arrives in earnest, will Jio try to build its own assistant (and risk another JioMeet), or sell its recharge screen to whoever wins?

I've put the full scorecard, three case studies and what would prove me wrong into a piece on Deconstructed. Link's in the first comment.

Which of these bets would you have called right? And which one caught you off guard?
